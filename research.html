What it asks

Examines how University of Dhaka students perceived the forced shift to electronic learning during COVID-19, and which technological, pedagogical, institutional and individual factors shaped their acceptance of it.

Approach

Cross-sectional online survey of 412 University of Dhaka students; closed questions analysed in SPSS, open questions coded thematically.

412respondents

Key findings

  • Performance expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, perceived usefulness and ease of use shape acceptance of e-learning.
  • Individual innovativeness and e-learning preparedness positively influence adoption.
  • Closing the digital divide, building digital literacy and involving students in e-learning policy could improve outcomes.

What it asks

Studies the culture of knowledge and information sharing among students living in 16 dormitories of a large public university in Bangladesh, and how it varies by gender, discipline and degree level.

Approach

Structured questionnaire; 337 usable responses from 16 dormitories; descriptive statistics, t-tests and ANOVA in SPSS.

337respondents

Key findings

  • Students share knowledge and information with peers frequently; about 30% do so “very often”.
  • 85.46% see knowledge and information sharing as important for career development.
  • Male students report sharing more often, and attitudes differ significantly by gender; discipline shows no significant difference.
  • Top barriers: low awareness of knowledge sharing and social media addiction.

What it asks

Uses webometrics to map worldwide usage and growth of Library Genesis and Sci-Hub, the shadow libraries that researchers turn to when scholarly publications are priced out of reach.

Approach

Webometric analysis using LibGen and Sci-Hub databases, SimilarWeb web analytics and Sci-Hub download logs, with quantitative and qualitative analysis (data to 2022).

Key findings

  • LibGen and Sci-Hub show a remarkable surge in popularity despite copyright disputes and restrictions.
  • Users come from many nations, including developed countries such as the USA.
  • Science and technology dominate downloads, with medicine the most sought-after field.
  • High prices and limited availability of scholarly publications are the two main drivers.

What it asks

Explores how religious information shapes the personal morality and political beliefs of Bangladeshi citizens, in a country where religion plays a central role in society and politics.

Approach

Online survey of 210 respondents (convenience sampling), five-point Likert scale, Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal–Wallis tests.

210respondents

Key findings

  • Religious information has an impact on Bangladeshi citizens' morality and political beliefs.
  • Opinions differ significantly by educational level, current residency and information literacy.
